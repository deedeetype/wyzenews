Iran Launches Massive Attacks on Gulf States, Disrupting Global Oil Supply

Iran’s sweeping drone and missile attacks mark a major escalation across the Gulf, widening the conflict beyond Israel and directly threatening the world’s most important energy corridor. Strikes reportedly hit Israeli air bases as well as oil tankers, airports, and facilities in Iraq, Oman, the UAE, Bahrain, Kuwait, and Saudi Arabia, raising fears of a broader regional war and a prolonged supply shock. The immediate market impact was severe: oil prices surged, prompting the International Energy Agency to release 400 million barrels from strategic reserves in an effort to calm supply concerns. The U.S. then responded by targeting Iranian air capabilities and mine-laying assets in the Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint for global crude shipments. What to watch next is whether shipping through Hormuz is further disrupted, whether Gulf states coordinate a military response, and whether emergency stock releases can offset sustained damage to energy infrastructure.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Iran launched large-scale strikes across the Gulf, hitting energy, transport, and military-linked targets in multiple countries.
  • Oil prices jumped sharply, leading the International Energy Agency to release 400 million barrels from reserves.
  • U.S. forces struck Iranian air and naval mine-laying assets, increasing the risk of wider regional escalation around the Strait of Hormuz.
